## Weekly Macroeconomic and Trading Overview (September 6–13, 2025)

### Global Macroeconomic Developments:
1. **United States:**
   - **Federal Reserve Expectations:** A weaker-than-expected August employment report, which highlighted non-farm payrolls expanding by just 22,000 and an increase in unemployment to 4.3%, has prompted expectations of a more aggressive cut in interest rates by the Federal Reserve. It's now anticipated that the Fed may cut rates by 50 basis points at the upcoming FOMC meeting.
   - **Decline in Consumer Sentiment:** The consumer sentiment index from the University of Michigan fell to 55.4 from 58.2, reflecting growing consumer pessimism.

2. **Eurozone:**
   - **Investor Morale:** The Sentix index demonstrated a significant drop in investor confidence, falling to -9.2, the lowest since April.

3. **Energy Markets:**
   - **OPEC+ Production Changes:** OPEC+ plans to raise oil output by 137,000 barrels per day in October, despite declining oil prices and a global oversupply.

4. **Emerging Markets:**
   - **Sovereign Debt Performance:** These markets recorded solid performance with high-yield sovereigns showing better returns compared to the investment grade.

5. **European Central Bank (ECB):**
   - **Macroeconomic Projections:** ECB projects a global GDP growth rate decline from 3.6% to 3.3% in 2025, continuing a downward trend into 2026.

6. **Fed's Influence on Global Economies:**
   - Impact is observed across different regions, affecting economic strategies and policies.
